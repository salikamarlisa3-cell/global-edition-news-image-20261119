**THE WORLD IN ONE DAY — THURSDAY 19 NOVEMBER 2026.** One newspaper front page, one image, the whole day: **52 numbered stories** about Thursday 19 November 2026, built for density and completeness. The lead is the first-ever three-way summit — the presidents of the United States, China and Russia meeting for 90 minutes on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen — drawn as a dusk skyline under the three flags. Sixteen more illustrated panels follow: the World Court's judgment in The Gambia v. Myanmar, a third attack on the Crimea Bridge, IG Metall's 24-hour strikes, the migrant boat lost off Senegal, COP31's final stretch in Antalya, the hawkish Fed minutes, Anthropic's Nasdaq debut as ANT, GTA VI's launch day, Giannis sinking his old team in Miami, Bangladesh's lockdown, the Saudi-led coalition turning back Houthi missiles over Riyadh, Venezuela's AI-for-oil pitch, and spotlights on the UNHCR's Malaysia halt, Indonesia's forfeiture law, the Ethiopian Airlines breach probe and the PHP critical release. A **dot-matrix world map** carries numbered pins that match the story numbers, so a reader can place the day before reading it. A dark **UTC timeline strip** runs under the masthead, and the page closes with **by-the-numbers** and **weather & disaster** strips, a **left out — and why** date-check box (Voyager 1's light-day was 18 November; the Gulf strikes were 17–18 November; one Siberia crash report stayed unverified), and **35 one-line briefs (stories 18–52)** sweeping the rest of the wires, each naming its outlet on the page. Every story is cited, outlet by outlet, in `SOURCES.md` — Reuters, AP, AFP, USA Today, the Independent, Al Jazeera, the EU Council and more, all reporting on 19 November 2026.

The artwork is original and drawn entirely in code: `main.py` is a self-contained Python program (standard library only — its own PNG writer, raster canvas and hand-drawn 5×7 pixel font) that renders the page from the story data and writes `entry.png`. No photographs, no copied art, no external assets, no network. Run it twice and the bytes are identical.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
